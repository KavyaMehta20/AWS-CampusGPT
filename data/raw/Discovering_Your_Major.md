Source: RIT Dubai, Business & Management, Academic Advising presentation "Discovering Your Major" (converted from a 27-slide PDF).

## Start with your official program curriculum
Your curriculum is your degree map. Use the RIT Dubai website and your official program plan, not screenshots, old copies, or another student's schedule.
How to find your program plan on the RIT Dubai website: 1. RIT Dubai, 2. Academics & Learning, 3. Undergraduate Degrees, 4. Your program.

## Your degree has two connected halves
Think of your degree as a combination of business specialization and university-wide learning.

MAJOR half of the degree:
- Core Courses: 11 courses
- Major Courses: program-specific
- Major Electives: choice within your field
- Open Electives: your broader choices

GENERAL EDUCATION half of the degree:
- Foundation courses: 2
- Perspectives: 8
- Immersion: 1 (3 courses)
- GenEd Required: 5
- GenEd Electives: 2
- Minimum 60 credits of General Education.

## General Education: the other half of your degree
General Education is not "extra." It broadens the way you think, communicate and interpret problems.
Structure: 2 Foundation courses, 8 Perspectives, 1 Immersion (3 courses), 5 required courses, 2 electives.
Minimum 60 credits across General Education requirements.

## General Education: Foundation courses
The two foundation courses are:
- UWRT 150 Writing Seminar
- One approved Islamic Culture course. Options: ANTH 275 Global Islam; ANTH 365 Culture and Politics in the Middle East; HIST 402 Seminar in Islamic History; ARTH 549 Topics in Global Art & Architecture: Islamic Art & Architecture.
Your foundation courses satisfy university-wide writing and UAE/MOE requirements.

## General Education: Perspectives (eight different lenses)
The goal is breadth: different ways of understanding people, society, science, ethics and the world.
- Artistic: ENGL 210 / 211 / 316
- Social: PSYC 101 / PHIL 305 / HIST 104
- Scientific Principles: BIOG 101 / CHMG 141 / PSYC 101
- Natural Science Inquiry: PHYS 111 / BIOG 140
- Mathematical A: STAT 145
- Mathematical B: STAT 146
- Ethical: MGMT 340
- Global: ECON 101
Course availability and approved options can change. Check the current program plan.

## General Education: Immersion (three courses in one area)
An immersion is a small concentration outside your main program. It is three courses in one area.
Immersion areas: Advertising & Public Relations; Communication; Creative Writing; Cultural Anthropology; Digital Literature & Comparative Media; Economics; Ethics; History; International Relations; Language Science; Literature & Media; Modern Languages (Arabic); Modern Languages (French); Modern Languages (Spanish); Mathematics; Philosophy; Psychology; Religious Studies; Sociology.
Use the immersion to broaden your degree, or potentially build toward a minor.

## Immersion planning: three details students often miss
- Arabic: ANTH 275 Global Islam or ANTH 365 Culture & Politics in the Middle East may also satisfy the Islamic Culture requirement.
- Economics: ECON 101 Microeconomics is a prerequisite and a Global Perspective; it does not count toward the Economics immersion.
- Psychology: PSYC 101 Introduction to Psychology is a prerequisite and may satisfy a Perspective; it does not count toward the Psychology immersion.
A course can appear relevant to more than one requirement without being allowed to double-count.

## Use your electives deliberately
Your choices can work together rather than sitting in separate boxes: GenEd / free electives plus an Immersion can lead toward a possible minor.
Important: some courses can satisfy more than one category in theory, but they cannot necessarily be counted twice. Your advisor can help you place the course correctly in your degree audit.

## General Education electives: breadth with rules
Choose from approved areas such as: History, Languages, Literature, Psychology, Philosophy, Mathematics, Economics, Science.
Rule 1, you cannot move backward. Example: do not take an easier mathematics course after completing Applied Calculus.
Rule 2, avoid courses that are too similar. Example: a Probability and Statistics course may not be appropriate if you are already taking Applied Statistics.

## Open electives: where you have the most freedom
Open electives are deliberately flexible, as long as prerequisites and degree rules are met.
Go deeper: take an extra course from your major; explore another field; or take a course from a different program.
Go broader: take an additional General Education course; build a minor; or use the credit toward an approved minor.
"Pretty much anything" still means: check prerequisites and degree applicability first.

## Co-op: cooperative education
Co-op turns the degree into work experience. Cooperative education is full-time, paid work directly related to your field of study.
- Standing: 3rd-year standing
- Preparation: complete SCBI 035 Careers in Business
- Duration: 1 or 2 summers = 16 weeks
- Academic status: good standing, minimum 2.0 GPA
Start planning early. Co-op is easier when you prepare before you are eligible.

## Advising is mandatory before you enroll each semester
Meet your Academic Advisor or Faculty Advisor before enrollment opens.
1. Review how the semester is going.
2. Plan the next semester's courses.
3. Resolve holds, questions and degree issues.
4. Book through Starfish.
Students have questions. Advisors have answers, and planning early usually gives you more options.

## Academic planning: see the whole degree, not just next semester
Your degree worksheet helps you see what is complete, what remains, and where you still have choices.
The worksheet covers four parts: Business Core (shared requirements); Major (required courses plus electives); General Education (foundation plus perspectives plus immersion); Open Electives (remaining flexibility).
Use it to ask: What have I completed? What is still required? Where do prerequisites matter? Where do I still have choice?
Your advisor helps you interpret the plan, not just fill boxes.

## Two advisors, different questions
ACADEMIC ADVISOR, go to them for:
- Overall academic guidance
- Graduation requirements and GenEd
- Enrollment / withdrawal support
- Policies and procedures
- Study abroad and academic opportunities
- Academic difficulty / Academic Action

FACULTY ADVISOR, go to them for:
- Questions about your major and career prospects
- Major elective selection
- Co-op approval and guidance
- Post-graduation plans
- Difficulty in major courses
- Possible research opportunities
Your assigned advisors may change as the department and programs develop. Check your current advising information rather than relying on an old list.
